Buying a Liquor License

The best purchase is not simply the lowest-priced license. It is the license that fits your business model, can be transferred where you need it, and closes under terms that protect you if approvals are delayed or denied.

Start with the business, not the listing

Step one

Define the privileges you need

Restaurant service, bar service, package sales, spirits, beer and wine, catering and other uses can require different license classes.

Step two

Confirm geography

Verify that the license can be used or transferred into the county, municipality or specific premises where you intend to operate.

Step three

Verify status

Understand whether the license is active, inactive, escrowed, encumbered or subject to regulatory issues before you commit funds.

Buyer due-diligence checklist

  • Exact license number, class and current owner
  • Transferability and geographic restrictions
  • Outstanding taxes, liens, disciplinary matters or claims
  • Zoning and local-use approval for the intended premises
  • Required ownership disclosures and background qualifications
  • Government filing fees, transfer fees and professional costs
  • Estimated approval timeline and closing contingencies
  • Whether the purchase contract allows deposit return if approvals fail

A safer transaction sequence

Negotiate a written agreement

Document price, deposit, contingencies, closing date, included rights and who bears which costs.

Place funds under controlled terms

Use an escrow or closing structure that states exactly when money may be released.

Submit transfer applications

Complete state and local filings promptly and respond to regulatory requests.

Close only when conditions are met

Coordinate regulatory approval, transfer documents and release of purchase funds.

Do not assume a listing description proves eligibility. The buyer should independently verify the license class, transfer rules, intended location and regulatory requirements before closing.
