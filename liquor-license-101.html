Liquor License 101

A liquor license is the legal authority a business needs to sell alcoholic beverages. The exact rights, transfer rules, availability and market value depend on the state, local jurisdiction and license class.

What a liquor license controls

Product

What may be sold

Some licenses permit beer and wine only, while others authorize spirits or full-liquor sales.

Location

Where sales may occur

Licenses may be tied to a premises, county, municipality or other defined licensing area.

Use

How alcohol may be sold

On-premise consumption, package sales, restaurant service, club use and other privileges can be regulated separately.

Why some licenses become valuable assets

In markets where the number of licenses is limited, an existing transferable license can have substantial market value. In other jurisdictions, licenses are issued administratively if the applicant and location satisfy the rules, so the license itself may have little or no resale value.

Key idea: the economic value of a liquor license comes from the rights attached to it, how scarce those rights are, where the license can be used and whether the license may be transferred.

The basic transaction

Identify the license

Confirm the license class, jurisdiction, status, location restrictions and transferability.

Agree on price and terms

Buyer and seller establish price, deposits, contingencies, closing conditions and responsibility for fees.

Apply for transfer

The buyer usually must qualify with the applicable state and local authorities before operating under the license.

Close after approvals

Funds, documents and the license transfer are coordinated so neither side takes unnecessary closing risk.

Questions to answer before you buy

  • Does this exact license class permit the business model you plan to operate?
  • Can the license be moved to your intended location?
  • Are there zoning, distance, seating, food-service or local approval requirements?
  • Is the license active, inactive, escrowed, suspended or subject to liens or claims?
  • What state and local approvals are required before closing?
