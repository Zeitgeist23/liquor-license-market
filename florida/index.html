Why Florida has a quota-license market

Florida does not issue an unlimited number of general liquor licenses. Under section 561.20, Florida Statutes, the number of quota licenses is limited by county population. The Division of Alcoholic Beverages and Tobacco explains that a quota license is generally required to sell liquor unless a business qualifies for a statutory special-license exception.

Because supply is limited and licenses can change hands in the private market, values can differ substantially from county to county. Population, business demand, available inventory, license status and transfer conditions can all affect the market.

Common Florida liquor-license categories

The exact license class matters. Florida uses different categories for on-premise consumption, package sales, beer and wine, special restaurant licensing and other uses. Two of the most searched transferable quota categories are 4COP and 3PS.

Quota Market

4COP

DBPR identifies 4COP as a beer, wine and liquor consumption-on-premises category. It is widely associated with bars, lounges, nightclubs, restaurants and other businesses that require full-liquor privileges, depending on the specific license and local approvals.

Package Sales

3PS

DBPR identifies 3PS as beer, wine and liquor package sales. It is commonly associated with liquor stores and other off-premise package-sales operations.

Non-Quota Alternative

Special Restaurant Licensing

Florida also provides special restaurant licensing outside the ordinary quota limitation when statutory eligibility requirements are satisfied. These licenses are different from quota licenses and carry their own operating conditions.

Local Market

County-by-County Value

A Florida quota license is tied to a county market. Availability and value can therefore differ significantly between Miami-Dade, Broward, Palm Beach, Orange, Hillsborough, St. Johns and other counties.

Florida liquor-license questions

Why can Florida quota licenses have substantial market value?

The number of quota licenses is limited by county population, while demand comes from businesses that need the privileges attached to those licenses. Private-market pricing is therefore driven by supply and demand rather than a state-set resale price.

Is every Florida restaurant required to buy a quota license?

No. Florida provides special restaurant licensing for qualifying businesses that meet statutory requirements. Whether a quota license or special restaurant license is appropriate depends on the business, premises and intended alcohol privileges.

Does LLM sell the Florida licenses shown on FLLM?

Not necessarily. Florida Liquor License Market includes marketplace inventory and may also reference listings or opportunities handled by sellers, brokers or other sources. Buyers should verify the current listing details and the party handling a transaction.

Can a Florida quota license be moved to another county?

Quota licenses are county-specific assets. Transfer, location and operating privileges are subject to Florida law, DBPR approval and applicable local requirements. Buyers should confirm the specific license record and proposed use before relying on a listing.
