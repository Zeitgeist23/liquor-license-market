License Types by State

There is no single national liquor license. Each state uses its own license classes, terminology and transfer rules. Start with the business activity you need, then identify the state license that grants those rights.

Common license categories

On-premise

Restaurants & bars

Licenses for alcohol consumed at the licensed premises. Some classes distinguish restaurants, taverns, hotels or entertainment venues.

Off-premise

Package & retail stores

Licenses for sealed alcoholic beverages sold for consumption elsewhere, often with separate rules for spirits versus beer and wine.

Limited privileges

Beer, wine & special use

Many states offer narrower license classes for beer and wine, clubs, caterers, manufacturers, wholesalers or temporary events.

How to compare two license classes

Compare the operating rights

  • Beer and wine versus full liquor
  • On-premise versus off-premise sales
  • Hours and service restrictions
  • Food-service or seating requirements

Compare the transfer economics

  • Quota or unrestricted issuance
  • Geographic transfer limits
  • Transfer fees and local approvals
  • Market supply and recent asking prices
Important: a familiar label such as “full liquor” can mean different things from one state to another. Always verify the exact statutory or administrative license class before relying on a listing description.
